Core Systems RFP Response

team project ✦ award winner
Description
This 57-page mock proposal was written as a response to a request for proposal (RFP 03-2026a) from a fictional company (BTB Heavy Equipment). The company requested about 250 standard desktop computers and wanted them by June 2026. Our mock company (Core Systems) carefully selected and justified every component, performed cost analysis for the build at $234,082.50 against a $250,000 budget, and planned to offer methods for data backup, installation, and ethical disposal of the old machines.
Skills used
Technical writingProject managementHardware selectionBudgeting
Sample

Read the full proposal (PDF, 57 pages) ✦ GitHub repository

Selected pages (click to enlarge):

My role & challenges

Our team of four excelled at this project. As the project manager, I immediately noticed that we had a well-balanced team and I promptly worked with my team to assign roles. I assisted with hardware component selection and compatibility, made sure project details consistently met project requirements, and wrote the majority of the proposal contents. I formatted and designed the structure of the document, and did extensive research to provide evidence for our claims and suggestions. I assisted my team when they had questions or needed help on the assigned sections.

One challenge the team solved: RAM prices had jumped, so we chose an appropriate module that met the requirements and a motherboard with dual-channel memory, so more RAM can be added later. We included a page on RAM considerations to ensure the mock client was well informed and could make intelligent business decisions.

Reflection

The provided sample is the finished proposal. Our proposal won Best in Class and Best in Program for technical communications. This award shows I can lead a team, turn a client's requirements into a costed and well-documented plan, and write for executives and technical staff in the same document. I created my own template from scratch on Microsoft Word and our team worked on this project from a shared document that we revised and reviewed multiple times before creating a final copy. When the final copy was created our team reviewed it a couple more times prior to submission.

How to Build: Lego Instructions Document

team project
Description
This is an illustrated instruction manual for building a Lego structure. This project demonstrates our attention to detail and ability to write technical documents. This instructions manual includes terms, materials, warnings, notes, and numbered steps to build the structure with figures to assist the reader/builder.
Skills used
Technical writingInstructional designDocument designTeamwork
Sample

Selected pages (click to enlarge). Read the full instructions (PDF, 10 pages).

GitHub repository

github.com/skycreated/Lego-Instructions-Document
The finished instruction manual and the assignment instructions, as PDFs.

My role & challenges

I worked with a team of four (including myself) for this project. I was in charge of the document formatting and the majority of the writing for the final copy. I utilized new software (studio 2.0 - Bricklink) to develop accurate digital Lego models for the figures and images throughout the document.

The manual was built for a reader who has never seen the structure. I included safety information in a "Before you begin" sidebar, marked warnings in yellow and notes in blue, and supplemented every build step with a numbered figure.

Reflection

Before I wrote this manual, most of my writing was research papers and essays. This assignment got me excited about technical writing and what that encompasses. I started writing more technical writing samples, and practicing manual design, proposal writing, and technical documentation and design because of the enjoyment this assignment provided me.
